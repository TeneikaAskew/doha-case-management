Here's the full provenance picture, verified against the pipeline in the repo — there are two distinct data layers, and it's worth keeping them separate when explaining to the stakeholder:

Layer 1: Real data — the DOHA decision corpus
The repo contains ~36,700 real, publicly published DOHA decisions (~28,650 hearings + ~8,050 appeals) scraped from doha.ogc.osd.mil using Playwright browser automation (the site's Akamai bot protection blocks plain HTTP, so scraping drives a real Chromium browser — the process is documented in DOHA_SCRAPING_GUIDE.md). PDFs were downloaded, parsed to text, and stored as parquet files in doha_parsed_cases/ using pandas/pyarrow.

This real corpus feeds exactly three things in the portal, via corpus.py:

Precedent citations on guideline cards — real case numbers, outcomes, and source URLs back to doha.ogc.osd.mil, selected deterministically (newest denied + granted per guideline)
One real decision document in the document viewer (a Guideline F hearing decision)
Analytics page statistics — grant/deny rates by guideline and year, computed from the actual corpus
These are public records; they contain no more PII than DOHA itself publishes.

Layer 2: Synthetic data — every subject, address, SSN, and incident
None of the 15 subjects comes from a DOHA case. The people, addresses, SSNs, DOBs, credit records, police reports, and alerts are entirely fabricated, generated by plain Python in portal/data_gen/ with pydantic schemas validating every record and pytest (30 tests) enforcing the invariants. No LLM and no randomness at generation time — the output is deterministic and reviewable in source.

Two generation methods:

3 "hero" cases (hero_cases.py) are hand-authored fictional narratives, written to be realistic under SEAD-4 — that's where the specific addresses come from. They were composed for geographic plausibility: residential addresses are invented street/number combinations in real localities (Manassas, Chesapeake, Columbia), while a few employer addresses are real public business addresses (e.g., Port of Virginia at 600 World Trade Center, Norfolk; GMU at 4400 University Dr) used as workplaces, not personal data.
12 roster subjects (roster.py) are assembled by deterministic index arithmetic over fixed lists of street names, cities, and employers — subject n always gets the same synthetic profile.
Deliberate fictional-safety conventions are baked in and test-enforced: 900-series SSNs (never issued by the SSA), 555-01xx phone numbers (reserved for fiction), and example-domain emails.

How the shape of the data was determined
The case structure was modeled on the real process, not on any individual's record: SEAD-4's thirteen Adjudicative Guidelines and nine whole-person factors, SF-86 section structure, the DCSA continuous-vetting alert flow, and disqualifier/mitigator citations that quote the actual Adjudicative Guidelines paragraphs (AG ¶ 19(a), ¶ 20(b), etc.). The design research behind those choices is written up in docs/superpowers/specs/.

One-sentence version for the stakeholder: real public DOHA decisions power the precedents and analytics; every subject and their personal details are deterministic synthetic fiction generated from schema-validated Python, designed so nothing in the demo can correspond to a real person.
